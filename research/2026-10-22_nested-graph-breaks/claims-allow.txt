- In the original retained depth-8 B200 compiler microbenchmark, running with nested graph-break support produced a 4.531794562480241x geometric-mean steady median latency ratio relative to running without it across four paired seeds. The individual ratios ranged from 4.495351630265558x to 4.579453627436775x.
- The original result compared the same 64 FP32 operations under PyTorch 2.14.1+cu130 and Inductor reduce-overhead mode.
- In that original cohort, the geometric-mean steady medians were 407.567 microseconds with nested graph-break support off and 89.935 microseconds with it on.

- Independent fresh run 022 measured a separate 4.5417580539950775x depth-8 steady geometric-mean ratio across four paired seeds. Its individual ratios ranged from 4.438828272326588x to 4.6530922469391465x.
- Fresh run 022 passed its steady measurement, steady control, correctness, and capture gates. Its startup measurement did not pass the fixed startup rule.
- Retained fresh022 Nsys evidence shows 10,160 versus 1,270 whole-capture CUDA Graph launch calls. The fixed post-warmup view shows 16 versus 2 graph executions for the selected invocation.

- Normal unprofiled workers establish the latency result. Nsys and NCU captures provide separate supporting mechanism evidence and include profiler overhead.

- Fewer and larger compiled regions are the leading explanation for the steady result.
- The larger candidate fused kernel does more work per launch.
- The retained profiles support an execution-structure explanation. They do not establish an exact CPU or GPU causal share.
